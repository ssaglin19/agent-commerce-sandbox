# Agent-commerce market notes

Dated notes on public-source reporting about agent payments, commerce, and trust. These entries summarize claims in the linked sources; secondary-source reporting is labeled.

## 2026-10-02

- **Agentic payment proof points and Verifiable Intent (secondary report):** The Agent Report describes controlled live agent-purchase proof points involving Worldline, ING, Mastercard and Visa in Europe, and notes these are not broad commercial availability. It reports that Verifiable Intent is intended to create tamper-resistant evidence of what a consumer authorized and is aligned with Google's AP2 and UCP. Source: [The Agent Report, "Agentic Commerce Leaves the Sandbox"](https://the-agent-report.com/2026/10/agentic-commerce-production-rails-2026/).

- **Mastercard Agent Pay trust and intelligence services:** Mastercard says it is testing in the US a probability score for whether a transaction was initiated by an AI agent, alongside planned identity, intent, behavioral and fraud signals for issuer and merchant authorization decisions. Mastercard names Cloudflare and Skyfire as collaborators. Source: [The Paypers, "Mastercard adds AI agent trust services to Agent Pay"](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/mastercard-expands-agent-pay-with-trust-and-intelligence-services).

- **Meow on business agent workflows:** Meow describes using its existing scoped spend controls, multi-entity accounts, corporate cards and payment features in business payment workflows involving agents. Source: [Meow, "Building Agentic Payment Workflows"](https://www.meow.com/blog/building-agentic-payment-workflows-meow-s-scoped-controls-make-it-safe-to-ship).

## 2026-10-03

- **EMVCo agentic payments framework consultation:** EMVCo closed public comments Sept. 30 on its proposed card-payment framework. Its proposed Intent Services would support registering, referencing, retrieving and managing consumer-authorized intent before, during and after transactions. EMVCo may also consider KYA capabilities and agentic transaction indicators; its representative said historical intent may be needed for dispute resolution. Feedback review is underway and future specifications are not yet decided. This is a standards proposal, not a launched feature. Source: [PYMNTS, "EMVCo Framework Targets Consumer Intent in Agentic Payments"](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/emvco-framework-targets-consumer-intent-agentic-payments/).

## 2026-10-04

- **CellCog Spend consumer purchasing launch:** CellCog says its Spend feature is live for AI employees. Users connect a Link wallet and approve each exact merchant and amount in Link; an approved purchase uses a one-time card. CellCog says multiple employees in one organization can share the wallet, with employee-specific purchase switches, optional caps, purchase histories and a Spend app view of purchases and status. This is a company-authored product description; it describes aggregation across CellCog's own employees, not independent third-party agents. Source: [CellCog, "Your AI Employees Can Now Buy Things, With Your OK"](https://cellcog.ai/blog/ai-employee-spend/).

## 2026-10-05

- **Snaplii Cash for AI-agent purchases:** Snaplii says users can fund a separate Snaplii Cash wallet, set a spending limit, and use the balance for purchases by agents without exposing their card or bank details. Snaplii says it works with Meta Muse today and is built for other consumer agents, including Instinct; purchases can earn cashback. It also describes an MCP-compatible Agent-to-Merchant interface with one-time prefunded credentials. These are company claims; the announcement does not establish consolidated transaction histories or dispute tools across independent agents. Source: [ACCESS Newswire, "Snaplii Offers a Separate Wallet for AI Agent Spending, With Cashback"](https://week.marketminute.com/article/accwirecq-2026-10-5-as-muse-tops-the-app-store-and-instinct-raises-1-billion-snaplii-offers-a-separate-wallet-for-ai-agent-spending-with-cashback).

- **EMVCo Intent Services (secondary technical analysis):** A technical analysis of EMVCo's draft describes Intent Services as a proposed registry/coordinator for consumer-authorized intent and order status, including cumulative budgets and post-transaction audit or dispute references. It emphasizes that the proposed service would not originate intent, authorize payments or enforce rules. This remains draft-framework analysis, not a launched capability. Source: [PCI Proxy, "What Are Intent Services in Agentic Payments?"](https://www.pci-proxy.com/blog-posts/emvco-intent-services-agentic-payments).

## 2026-10-06

- **Visa tokens tied to purchasing intent:** A PYMNTS interview with Visa's product executive says Visa agentic tokens are generated against a defined purchasing intent, constrain credential use, and pair with FIDO-based authentication, including biometrics. The report also describes Visa token management correlating credentials across an online agent purchase and an in-store return/refund when Gemini and the merchant used different payment paths. This indicates payment-network-level intent binding and post-purchase reconciliation; the source is a PYMNTS article republished by Bytes Europe. Source: [Bytes Europe, "AI Agents Bring Shoppers, but Merchants Must Close the Sale"](https://www.byteseu.com/2427997/).

## 2026-10-07

- **OpenMarket paid Plus tier:** OpenMarket documents a paid Plus plan for its market terminal. Its guide says Free has one chart per layout, three indicator slots per chart, five active alerts and reduced order-book depth; Plus allows 16 charts, 10 indicator slots and 50 active alerts, with private indicators, deeper order-book tools, one-second charts and Hyperliquid wallet overlays. The company says both plans receive the same live data. This is a monetization move in adjacent market tooling, not evidence yet of paid agent-generated signals or user traction. Source: [OpenMarket Plus, plans and limits](https://openmarket.xyz/learn/getting-started/plans-and-limits). Its API also documents $99/$199/$599 monthly tiers for larger request budgets/history and selected advanced features: [OpenMarket API rate limits](https://openmarket.xyz/docs/rate-limits).

- **Consumer permission preferences and infrastructure framing:** PYMNTS reports survey results in which 56% of consumers would allow an agent to search and compare, but 35% would grant access to saved payment credentials; for a hypothetical $300 purchase, 52% wanted to select the payment method, while 25% would accept an AI recommendation but keep approval. The article frames networks, issuers, processors and acquirers as potential interoperable permission infrastructure. Source: [PYMNTS, "AI Agents Turn Permission Into Payments' New Control Point"](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/ai-agents-turn-permission-into-payments-new-control-point/).

## 2026-10-08

- **Crossmint Agent Commerce Toolkit launch:** Crossmint says its live, self-serve developer toolkit combines card collection and storage, Visa Intelligent Commerce and Mastercard Agent Pay credentials for eligible cards, and merchant checkout in one integration. For cards that cannot use those network credentials, Crossmint says the toolkit can pay securely from its PCI-compliant vault. It selects among Shop Pay, Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP), and browser automation according to availability, reliability and cost; an open-source reference app demonstrates the flow with live purchases. This packages authorization, card rails and checkout orchestration for developers, strengthening platform competition in agent checkout while leaving the consumer cross-agent spending dashboard question open. Source: [Crossmint via PR Newswire, "Crossmint Launches Agent Commerce Toolkit"](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/crossmint-launches-agent-commerce-toolkit-enabling-ai-agents-to-pay-with-any-card-and-check-out-online-through-one-integration-302901649.html).

## 2026-10-09

- **Rezolve Pay targets checkout across consumer agents:** Rezolve Ai says its merchant-side agent and payment infrastructure can accept orders from any consumer AI assistant through compatible merchant integrations. A company video shows Meta Muse ordering coffee for in-store pickup through a Rezolve-enabled merchant. The announcement is a cross-agent merchant-acceptance demonstration, not evidence of broad merchant deployment or independent adoption; it is adjacent competition at the checkout layer, rather than a consumer-side spend dashboard. Source: [Rezolve Ai, "Rezolve Ai Opens Retail to Any AI Shopping Agent with Rezolve Pay"](https://rezolve.com/press-releases/rezolve-ai-opens-retail-to-any-ai-shopping-agent-with-rezolvepay-targeting-a-multi-trillion-dollar-commerce-opportunity/).

- **Ant Digital Technologies and HSBC micropayment test:** The companies say a technical verification test combined HSBC's Tokenised Deposit Service and real-time risk checks through its MCP interface with Ant's Anvita Flow and Jovay testnet. The demonstration connected agent service discovery and invocation with real-time-settled micropayments using tokenised deposits. The release explicitly says this was exploratory, not a commercial launch or live customer proposition. Source: [Ant Digital Technologies and HSBC via PR Newswire, "Successful AI Agent Micropayment Technical Verification Test"](https://www.prnewswire.com/apac/news-releases/ant-digital-technologies-and-hsbc-announce-successful-ai-agent-micropayment-technical-verification-test-302903333.html).

- **Hedera Agent Accounts announced:** Hedera describes programmable, non-custodial spending accounts with user-defined on-chain rules for agent payments, service access and purchases. It says limited partner validation on testnet is planned for Q4 2026, public testnet for Q1 2027 and mainnet for Q2 2027, subject to security review and legal sign-off. This is planned infrastructure, not a live consumer product. Source: [Hedera, "Introducing Hedera Agent Accounts"](https://hedera.com/blog/introducing-hedera-agent-accounts/).

## 2026-10-10

- **Personal Agent Protocol draft and expanded design partners:** Sierra published a draft of Personal Agent Protocol (Poppy), developed with Meta and others, and added 35 design partners including Adyen, Bank of America, Chime, Mastercard, OpenAI, PayPal, Plaid, Visa, Venmo and Wells Fargo. The draft centers on identifying personal agents, consumer-approved account access and business-defined boundaries; the protocol's overview says payments could be added as an extension, so this is not yet a payment or checkout standard. The partner group and draft are a platform-level move that could shape cross-agent authentication and permissions; they strengthen ecosystem competition but do not provide cross-agent spend aggregation or dispute-grade receipts. Source: [Sierra, "Sharing a draft of Personal Agent Protocol"](https://sierra.ai/blog/poppy); [Poppy overview](https://personalagentprotocol.org/docs/overview).

## 2026-10-11

- **Manus raises over $500 million as its Cue app gives personal agents wallets:** Manus's parent Butterfly Effect said it raised more than $500 million, led by Boyu Capital and IDG Capital; the company did not disclose a valuation. Manus's recent Cue launch describes agents with their own wallets that can pay within user-set budgets, with users controlling which actions need approval. The funding is a platform-side signal that adds resources behind consumer agents capable of spending. Cue's announcement does not describe cross-agent spend aggregation or dispute-grade receipts, so those consumer-control needs remain open. Sources: [TechCrunch, "China's Manus raises over $500M in first funding round since split with Meta"](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/chinas-manus-raises-over-500m-in-first-funding-round-since-split-with-meta/); [Manus, "Introducing Manus 2.0"](https://manus.im/blog/introducing-manus-2-0).

## 2026-10-12

- **Oobit AI Payments connects multiple consumer assistants to one-time stablecoin-funded cards:** Oobit says users can connect its account to supported AI assistants, approve each purchase and have Oobit issue a unique one-time card without exposing the underlying account or balance. Its product page names Meta Muse, Grok Bot and Dots as supported and labels Instinct "Soon." The company says the service is beginning to roll out with selected assistants and partners. This is a consumer-side wallet and approval option across several assistants, validating cross-agent demand but limited to funds routed through Oobit; it does not establish a view of spending across unrelated payment sources or dispute-grade receipts. Sources: [Oobit, "Muse, Grok, Dots, and Instinct Assistants Can Now Spend Stablecoins"](https://www.oobit.com/news/muse-grok-dots-and-instinct-assistants-can-now-spend-stablecoins); [Oobit AI Payments](https://www.oobit.com/ai-payments).

- **Revolut reportedly plans agentic shopping in AIR:** Sifted reported on Oct. 9 that Revolut intends to add shopping actions to its AIR in-app financial assistant. Revolut's public AIR page describes a current assistant for transactions, cards, subscriptions, investments and travel planning, but does not confirm shopping availability, timing or transaction controls. If shipped, this could bring shopping and payment approvals into an incumbent consumer-finance app; for now it is a reported product direction, not a verified shopping launch. Sources: [Sifted, "Revolut to roll out agentic shopping as AI commerce takes off"](https://sifted.eu/articles/revolut-agentic-shopping); [Revolut AIR](https://www.revolut.com/air-ai-by-revolut/).

## 2026-10-13

- **American Express Intent Intelligence Framework:** Amex announced an intent-focused addition to its Agentic Commerce Experiences (ACE) toolkit. TechCrunch reports that the proposed flow records a consumer's purchase instructions and constraints (such as merchant, maximum budget, and product preferences), authenticates the user, then creates an Intent ID and Proof of Intent Token used to obtain a secure payment credential. Amex says the identifiers can carry intent through purchase processing and support fraud and dispute resolution; it argues that a mismatch between the approved product and the agent's checkout request could be stopped before authorization. Amex says it has not yet seen an agentic-commerce dispute increase and is discussing ACE implementation with ecosystem partners; no broad deployment or implementation partner was named in the report. This is a significant large-network move toward binding structured consumer intent to payment credentials and dispute evidence. It validates the need for transaction-level authorization records, while leaving open whether consumers will get an independent cross-agent history or accessible dispute evidence. Source: [TechCrunch, "Amex introduces a framework to prevent AI agents from making unauthorized purchases"](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/13/amex-introduces-a-framework-to-prevent-ai-agents-from-making-unauthorized-purchases/).

- **Causa Prima raises $20 million for B2B agent-managed payment disputes:** Fortune reports that Causa Prima raised a $20 million seed round led by Base10, after a $10 million pre-seed, for software in which agents communicate between companies to resolve billing disputes, negotiate payment terms and early-payment discounts, with a fee on successfully negotiated early payments. It targets company accounts payable/receivable, not consumer purchases. The round is not a close consumer-dashboard competitor, but dispute-focused capital validates the broader need for auditable agent actions and resolution workflows; the consumer wedge remains distinct. Source: [Fortune, "Startup Causa Prima raises $20 million to tackle billing disputes with AI agents"](https://fortune.com/2026/10/13/startup-causa-prima-20-million-billing-disputes-ai-agents/).

- **Kudos 3.0 shifts a consumer finance app from tracking toward delegated money tasks:** Kudos's Oct. 12 Product Hunt launch describes a consumer home screen consolidating savings/rewards and a ranked next action, plus AI that activates card offers, calls providers to negotiate bills and cancels subscriptions; the company says users keep all savings and it does not move money without approval. Its existing site says it connects multiple cards, analyzes transactions and consolidates card offers/benefits. This is a consumer-finance pivot toward action plus a unified financial view, but its disclosed focus is rewards and money-saving tasks rather than agent purchase aggregation, agent authorization records or payment disputes. It is a nearby validation and potential channel competitor, not evidence of a direct cross-agent spend dashboard. Sources: [Kudos 3.0 on Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/kudos-smart-wallet); [Kudos](https://joinkudos.com/).

## 2026-10-14

- **Shopify opens Shop checkout and order lookup to MCP-compatible agents:** Shopify's developer changelog says its Shop MCP is live, with 17 tools for searching a catalog of more than one billion products, building carts, Shop Pay checkout, and order search. For Shopify-store checkout, a signed-in shopper's agent completes the order after the shopper confirms the total and payment method; for non-Shopify stores, the shopper approves in Shop and the agent receives single-use card details. Shop's help center also describes optional weekly, monthly, or total spending budgets that let a connected personal agent purchase without manual approval. This is a significant platform move combining broad agent access, buyer approval/budgets, checkout, and Shop order visibility. It validates approval-to-charge controls and gives Shopify an opportunity to own the consumer-facing commerce and order surface. The cited sources describe Shop-connected purchases, not an independent view across unrelated agent accounts or payment sources. Shopify's current help terms also restrict the personal-agent Shop skill to individual use and prohibit building commercial services or aggregating data from multiple Shop users; the policy distinguishes commercial integrations through its developer documentation. Sources: [Shopify developer changelog, "Add shopping and Shop Pay checkout to any agent with Shop MCP"](https://shopify.dev/changelog/posts/add-shopping-and-shop-pay-checkout-to-any-agent-with-shop-mcp); [Shop Help Center, "Using Shop with AI agents and agentic platforms"](https://help.shop.app/en/shop/shopping/personal-agents); [The Verge, "Shopify opens its catalog up to more AI agents"](https://www.theverge.com/news/1010365/shopify-opens-its-catalog-up-to-more-ai-agents).

- **AWS and Anchor Browser bring x402 payments into managed browser sessions:** An AWS post dated Oct. 13 describes an integration between Anchor Browser and Amazon Bedrock AgentCore payments that lets browser agents handle x402 payment challenges for paid web content and APIs. The workflow applies configured session budgets, uses a connected Coinbase CDP or Stripe Privy wallet, retries a paid request within the same browser session, and records payment attempts, approvals, denials, and settlements through AWS observability. AWS says the integration is available through AWS Marketplace; Anchor's docs label its automatic x402 browser feature as preview and say enabling it authorizes real payments. This is a meaningful infrastructure/distribution milestone for x402 and metered agent access, but it is developer/enterprise infrastructure rather than a consumer approval app; session budgets and operational audit logs do not by themselves create a consumer cross-agent purchase history or dispute-resolution surface. Sources: [AWS, "Building agents that transact with Anchor Browser and AgentCore payments"](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/building-agents-that-transact-with-anchor-browser-and-agentcore-payments/); [Anchor Browser, "Automatic x402 Payments"](https://docs.anchorbrowser.io/advanced/automatic-x402-payments); [AWS, AgentCore payments FAQs](https://aws.amazon.com/bedrock/agentcore/faqs/).

## 2026-10-15

- **Ledger Agent Intent launches a shared approval queue across agents:** Ledger says Agent Intent puts approval requests from different agents and harnesses into one interface, including examples involving Grok Bot, Venice AI, OpenClaw, Muse and others. The user approves on a Ledger signer; agents may continue asynchronously, and the company describes budgeted micro-payments (including x402 and MPP) without per-payment prompts within the user's limit. Ledger says Agent Intent and its developer portal are available now on existing signers. This is a notable consumer-side cross-agent approval surface, making it the closest new dashboard/control competitor in today's sweep. Its scope is a hardware-backed approval queue and configured agent actions; the post does not establish a unified transaction history across unrelated cards, bank accounts or non-Ledger payment activity. Source: [Ledger, "Ledger Agent Intent: A Single Interface for All Agent Requests"](https://www.ledger.com/blog-introducing-ledger-agent-intent).

- **Proof announces x401 for signed human authorization of agent actions:** Proof says its x401 protocol and MCP server are available to let a service request verified identity and a cryptographic signature over the specific transaction terms, such as amount, recipient and other mandate constraints; the signed evidence can be independently checked and retained for later disputes. The launch release claims production use and support for Muse, ChatGPT, Grok and Claude. However, Proof's x401 product page still labels v0.1.0 "Active development" and describes early adopters as building, with formal announcements forthcoming. Treat the scope and production deployment claims as vendor-reported and not yet independently corroborated. If delivered as described, x401 directly strengthens transaction-level authorization evidence, but is identity/authorization infrastructure rather than a consumer-facing cross-agent spend history. Sources: [Proof launch release](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/10/15/3381718/0/en/proof-launches-x401-giving-ai-agents-a-way-to-prove-who-they-represent-and-what-they-re-authorized-to-do.html); [Proof x401](https://proof.com/x401).

- **Stripe Link and Wix Symphony demonstrate approval-bound agent purchases for businesses:** Wix says Symphony agents can request a purchase, show the merchant and exact checkout total, request Link approval, then use a single-use credential to complete checkout without exposing the underlying card to the agent. Wix says a changed total or new purchase requires a new approval and that Symphony checks payment status with Link before retrying. This is a concrete platform integration for connecting human approval to the charged transaction, and a business-use validation of exact-amount controls; it is not presented as a general consumer agent-spend dashboard. Source: [Wix, "Symphony by Wix and Link, built by Stripe: how AI agents can now make purchases for your business"](https://www.wix.com/blog/symphony-wix-link-stripe-ai-agent-payments).

- **PayPal announces native checkout integration with Glance's consumer shopping assistant:** Glance says its integration with PayPal brings product data, cart and in-app checkout into Glance, so consumers can discover products and purchase without leaving the experience. This is a consumer distribution and checkout move by a major payment platform and shopping assistant. The announcement does not specify approval controls, cross-agent purchase aggregation or transaction-level intent evidence, so it validates consumer agentic commerce more than the dashboard/receipt proposition. Source: [Business Wire, "Glance and PayPal Bring AI-Powered Shopping and Checkout to Tens of Millions of U.S. Consumers"](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261015403540/en/Glance-and-PayPal-Bring-AI-Powered-Shopping-and-Checkout-to-Tens-of-Millions-of-U.S.-Consumers).
